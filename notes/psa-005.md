## 5:7

> 因着我的罪恶过犯，我在神面前毫无指望，唯有等候灭亡。我没有功劳可言，也不能凭自己拯救自己，更无法赎回自己的罪恶，只能原地等候审判之日来到。正如哈巴谷先知所说，我只可安静等候灾难之日临到，犯境之民上来。

> 然而，祢没有向我掩面——也就是没有对我隐藏祢的慈爱——我就敢单单因着祢的慈爱来到祢面前。而祢慈爱的终极表现就是基督，即祢的独生爱子。我因此而有指望，虽然凭自己我毫无指望。

> 我存着敬畏的心，向着祢为选民所留的慈爱——基督——下拜，因为祂的身体就是神的殿。在祢圣善的灵的引领下，带着对真理的些微窥见，我向这圣殿下拜。

> Because of my sins and transgressions, I have no hope before God and can only await destruction. I have no merit to speak of, nor can I save myself or redeem my own sins; I can only wait in place for the day of judgment to arrive. As the prophet Habakkuk said, I can only quietly wait for the day of calamity when the invading people come up.

> Yet you have not hidden your face from me—that is, you have not withheld your steadfast love from me—so I dare to come before you solely because of your steadfast love. And the ultimate expression of that love is Christ, your only-begotten Son. Therefore I have hope, even though in myself I have none.

> With a reverent heart I bow toward the steadfast love you have reserved for your elect—Christ—because his body is the temple of God. Led by your Holy Spirit, and with only a slight glimpse of the truth, I worship toward this temple.

## 5:8

> 倘若我最大的仇敌、那最凶恶、最绊倒我的，是外在的仇敌，我就还有可抱怨、可指责的对象，也能稍微减轻自己的责任感。可是如果那最凶恶的仇敌是我无法指责的呢？就是旧人绊倒新人，那旧的成了新人的拦阻。

> 可是新人并不亏欠旧人什么。新人也不亏欠这个世界什么。罗马书 8:12

> [12](https://wd.bible/verse/rom.8.12.cunps)

> 弟兄们，这样看来，我们并不是欠肉体的债去顺从肉体活着。

> 新人只以神为乐、为居所，以神的旨意为乐，以神的话语——道——为乐、为饼和水，以为这福音作见证为乐。

> 既然如此，按照神的公义，神要救这新人到底，因为这新人也是基督宝血赎买回来的，好让我能沿着基督这条道路一直走到父神那里去。

> If my greatest enemy—the most evil one that trips me up the most—were an external enemy, I would still have someone to complain about and blame, and could somewhat ease my own sense of responsibility. But what if the fiercest enemy is one I cannot blame? It is the old self tripping up the new self; the old has become a hindrance to the new.

> Yet the new self owes the old self nothing. The new self also owes the world nothing. Romans 8:12 So then, brothers, we are debtors, not to the flesh, to live according to the flesh.

> The new self finds its joy and dwelling only in God, delights only in God’s will, delights in God’s word—the Word—as its bread and water, and delights in bearing witness to this gospel.

> Since this is the case, according to God’s righteousness, God will save this new self to the end, because this new self has also been bought back by the blood of Christ, so that I may walk along this road of Christ all the way to God the Father.

> 耶稣说：“我就是道路、真理、生命。”

> 这条道路是通向神的得救之路，也是认识神的路。它的终点是基督——父怀里的独生子——“你们看见了我，就是看见了父”。然而父比万有都大，我们何时能完全认识父呢？我们也永远不能完全认识基督。因为我们若完全认识基督，就完全认识了父。我们无论认识多久，仍然有限，而神是无限。有限在无限面前始终可以算作无有。

> 所以这条路虽然有终点，却像永远无尽的路。但这不仅不让我们灰心，反倒让我们充满希望与探索的喜乐。正如人不会厌倦喜乐、财富、资源、平安，我们岂不是希望这些美好的事物永无穷尽吗？那么，赐下各样美善的恩赐和全备赏赐的神没有限量，岂不正是我们的快乐和希望吗？

> 如果有人说有其他得救的路，可是路的终点如果不是基督，那就不是到神那里去。无论描绘的前景多么美好——如同极乐世界、某种主义、某种理想、某种科学、某种宗教，或某种修炼之法——如果不是通向基督，那就一定是断头路。基督只有一位，神只有一位，所以这条路只有一条。是基督自己，终点也是祂。

> 何以知道我们真在这条路上呢？有真理的圣灵引导，有圣经作见证。我们喜爱真理，愿意明白真理，尽管有许多难以理解的地方。真理是我们的食物，尽管我们不明白食物的材料如何长成、营养如何被身体吸收，但我们天然喜爱食物，除非我们生病了，或者死了。如果我们以为自己走在这条路上，却对真理不感兴趣，我们就当怀疑：我们是否真的在这条路上，还是只喜爱神迹、奇事，或今生的好处。

> 如果我们走在这条路上，又喜爱真理，那是因为我们有从神来的生命。这生命天然喜爱通向基督的路，也天然喜爱真理。这生命的成长，其终点正如这条路的终点，要满有基督的样式，就荣耀神的名。

> **English revised version:**

> Jesus said, “I am the way, the truth, and the life.”

> This way is the path of salvation that leads to God and to the knowledge of God. Its destination is Christ—the only-begotten Son in the bosom of the Father—“Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.” Yet the Father is greater than all; when could we ever fully know the Father? We can never fully know Christ either. For if we fully knew Christ, we would fully know the Father. No matter how long we know him, we remain finite, while God is infinite. The finite before the infinite can always be counted as nothing.

> Therefore, although this way has a destination, it is like a road that never ends. Yet this does not discourage us; on the contrary, it fills us with hope and the joy of endless exploration. Just as people never grow weary of joy, wealth, resources, and peace, do we not desire that these good things be without end? Then is not the God who gives every good and perfect gift without measure our joy and our hope?

> If someone claims there is another way of salvation, yet the destination of that way is not Christ, then it does not lead to God. No matter how beautiful the future it depicts—whether a paradise, some ideology, some ideal, some science, some religion, or some method of cultivation—if it does not lead to Christ, it is a dead end. There is only one Christ and only one God; therefore there is only one way. Christ himself is both the way and the destination.

> How do we know we are truly on this way? We have the Spirit of truth to guide us, and the Scriptures bear witness. We love the truth and desire to understand it, even though there are many things we find difficult to grasp. Truth is our food. Even though we do not understand how its ingredients grow or how its nutrients are absorbed in the body, we naturally love food—unless we are sick or dead. If we think we are on this way but have no interest in the truth, we should question whether we are truly on it, or whether we only love signs, wonders, or the benefits of this present life.

> If we are on this way and love the truth, it is because we have life from God. This life naturally loves the way that leads to Christ and naturally loves the truth. The goal of this life’s growth, just like the destination of this way, is to be filled with the likeness of Christ, to the glory of God’s name.

## 5:9

> 这些不正是我们旧人的情形吗？
> 别人犯罪跌倒，与我何干？
> 各人岂不都要担当自己的罪吗？
> 旧人里面还能出什么好的呢？
> 他不能得神的喜悦，也不以神为乐；
> 他只专爱自己，专心以世界为念，
> 以世界为家乡，以坟墓为归宿。
> 他不能体贴神的心意，不以神为自己的居所，
> 不爱神的道，也不以福音为是。
> 他自然会落入这般光景，且理直气壮，
> 因为他单以地里所出的一切为食物。
> 如同蛇一样，是受了咒诅的。
> 也只有地里所出的，才能满足他的需要：
> 饮食、穿戴、金银、宝器。
> 他不能进入永恒，也不渴求永恒，
> 因为衰老和疾病甚至会让他厌弃他自己。
> 他所行的一切，他都不以为罪，
> 全是为了他自己今生的、暂时的好处。
> 若无好处可图，
> 甚至骨肉亲情都可以舍弃，
> 正如犹大出卖基督一样。
> 若不是还有良心的谴责作为约束，他定会毫无愧疚与顾忌地去做一切想做且对自己有利的事，
> 正如那句话所言：“我死后，哪管它洪水滔天”。
> 所以耶稣说，
> 约翰福音 6:63：
> “叫人活着的乃是灵，肉体是无益的；我对你们所说的话就是灵，就是生命。”

> Are these not the exact conditions of our old man?
> What does it matter to me if others sin and stumble?
> Will not each person bear their own sin?
> Can any good come out of the old man?
> He cannot please God, nor does he delight in God;
> He loves only himself, setting his mind entirely on the world,
> Taking the world as his homeland and the grave as his final destination.
> He cannot comprehend the will of God, nor does he make God his dwelling place.
> He does not love the Word of God, nor does he regard the gospel as truth.
> He naturally behaves this way, and does so with self-justification,
> Because he feeds solely on all that comes from the dust of the earth.
> Like the serpent, he is cursed.
> Only that which comes from the earth can satisfy his needs:
> Food, clothing, silver, gold, and precious vessels.
> He cannot enter eternity, nor does he seek it,
> Because aging and illness will even cause him to loathe himself.
> Everything he does, he does not consider as sin;
> It is all for his own temporary, earthly benefit.
> If there is no benefit to be gained,
> He would even abandon his own flesh and blood,
> Just as Judas betrayed Christ.
> Were it not for the condemnation of his conscience acting as a restraint, he would do everything he desires and everything advantageous to himself without the slightest guilt or hesitation,
> Just as the saying goes, "After me, the deluge."
> Therefore, Jesus said in John 6:63:
> "It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh profits nothing. The words that I speak to you are spirit, and they are life."

## 5:10

他虽是祢造的，却不认识祢，不接待祢，
也不能认识祢。
他以犯罪为戏耍，
只求人的荣耀，却不求从独一之神而来的荣耀；
专心以地上的事为念，
却不以天上的事为念。
祢理当逐出他，如同逐出以实玛利，
因为凭血气生的，断不能与凭应许而生的同得产业。
他没有真智慧，却多有诡计；
多生巧计，而毫无诚实。
倘若祢任凭他，他必在自己的罪中滑落得更深。
因此，祢使他的计谋破产，反倒是对他的一种拯救，
如同在毒疮尚未溃烂扩散前便将其切除。
他虽背叛祢，无故地与祢为敌，
祢的属性却不改变，仍以公义和慈爱待他。
如同手术刀与医药对身体的作用，
尽管会带来暂时的痛苦，却能止住疾病无尽的疯狂，这正是祢的良善。
Though he was created by You, he does not know You, nor does he receive You,
And indeed, he cannot know You.
He makes a sport of sinning,
Seeking glory from men rather than the glory that comes from the only God.
He sets his mind entirely on earthly things,
Rather than on the things that are above.
You ought to cast him out, just as Ishmael was cast out,
For he who is born of the flesh can never share the inheritance with him who is born of the promise.
He possesses no true wisdom, yet he is full of deceit;
He breeds cunning schemes, but has no truth in him.
If You were to give him over to himself, he would inevitably slip further into his own sin.
Therefore, when You bring his schemes to ruin, it is actually a form of salvation for him,
Like removing a poisonous ulcer before it grows any larger.
Although he betrays You and makes himself Your enemy without cause,
You remain unchanging, still dealing with him in both righteousness and love.
Just as a scalpel and medicine act upon the body,
Though they bring temporary pain, they are the very goodness that halts the endless madness of the disease.

## 5:11-12

> 凡父所赐给我的人，必到我这里来；到我这里来的，我总不丢弃他。出于神的，必听神的话。你们不听，因为你们不是出于神。只是你们不信，因为你们不是我的羊。

> 到基督这里来的，是从神生的，他们在患难中也能欢欢喜喜。既以这世界为客旅，其中多有痛苦、患难、悲伤与试炼，那么终点便不是令人恐惧的，反倒是令人渴慕的。因为这意味著脱离地上的帐棚，回到永远的家乡。在平安顺利时感恩且警醒；在患难险阻、死荫幽谷中明白这必是神的美意，从而更加感谢神。

> 原本生命毫无指望，连快乐过后也是加倍的空虚。豪华房屋与佳美装饰，都不过是片刻安慰，随之而来的便是极大失落。虽家财万贯，所求不过一日三餐；虽房屋万间，躺下不过三尺之宽。过去在死亡的恐惧中终日惴惴、不得安眠，如今却可以在父神面前得享安息。

> 祢以公义对待那因信称义之人；他们的指望如同灵魂的锚，通入幔内，使他们可以坦然无惧地来到施恩的宝座前，求怜恤、蒙恩惠。祢所爱的，祢必保守，且保守到底。经上又说：“妇人焉能忘记她吃奶的婴孩？即或有忘记的，我却不忘记你。”

> All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out. Whoever is of God hears the words of God. The reason why you do not hear them is that you are not of God. But you do not believe because you are not among my sheep.

> Those who come to Christ are born of God, and they can rejoice even in tribulations. Since we treat this world as a pilgrimage, filled with pain, tribulation, sorrow, and trials, the destination is not something to be feared, but rather something to be desired. For it means departing from this earthly tent to return to our eternal home. Be thankful and watchful in times of peace and prosperity; in times of hardship, danger, and the valley of the shadow of death, understand that this must be God's good will, and thus give thanks to Him even more.

> Originally, life was entirely without hope, and even fleeting happiness was followed by a profound emptiness. Luxurious houses and beautiful decorations offer nothing but momentary comfort, leading only to a tremendous sense of loss. Though one may possess immense wealth, he needs no more than three meals a day; though one may own ten thousand houses, he needs no more than a three-foot space to sleep. Formerly trembling all day in the fear of death and unable to find sleep, we can now find true rest in the presence of God the Father.

> You deal in justice with those justified by faith; their hope is like an anchor for the soul, entering the inner place behind the curtain, so that they may with confidence draw near to the throne of grace, that they may receive mercy and find grace. Those whom You love, You will preserve, and preserve to the very end. As it is also written: "Can a woman forget her nursing child? Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you."
