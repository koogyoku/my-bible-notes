# 诗篇 第9章

## 9:1

#### 神的奇妙作为，就是福音
所谓“称谢”和“传扬”的，正是神为自己的选民所预备，并亲自成就的救恩。
因为祂是始，也是终。祂并不是因为最终完成了什么，才成为“终”；乃是因为祂本来就是始，是终，是永恒不改变的神。
我们往往只有等待一个行动、一个工程结束以后，才知道它最终的结果；但我们的主不是这样。祂的旨意并不是随着历史的发展才逐渐确定。救赎有起点，有过程，也有终点，但神自己并不受这个过程的限制。祂从起初就知道终局，也照着自己永恒的旨意施行这一切。
若神自己有所限制，若基督只是一个有限的受造者，那么祂就不能以自己的位格承担神永恒的救赎旨意。因为受造者可以执行所领受的命令，却不能因此成为神本身。
基督之所以能够完全成就救恩，不是因为祂只是一个能力特别大的受造者，而是因为祂就是永恒的神子，与父同质。祂所成就的救恩，是三一神自己的救赎工作。
人的问题也不在于人的心意本身是无限的。人是受造者，因此人的理智、知识和能力仍然有限；但人具有不断认识、思想、追求和探索的能力，并且人的心能够不断越过眼前的受造之物，追求更高、更大的对象。这使我们看见，人确实具有神的形象。
然而，罪使人的意志失去了正确的方向。
堕落的天使也是受造者，他们并不是无限的；但他们本来所领受的理智和意志，却能够转向错误的对象。人的堕落也是如此。人不是因为拥有了某种无限的本性而犯罪，而是在有限的受造位置上，竟然要越过神所设立的界限，想要“像神一样”。
伊甸园中的试探正是从这里开始：
“你们吃的日子眼睛就明亮了，你们便如神能知道善恶。”
私欲既怀了胎，就生出罪来；罪既长成，就生出死来。
所以，亚当和夏娃并不是吃下果子以后，死亡才突然从无到有。罪在人的意志中有一个从诱惑、私欲、同意到实际悖逆的过程，而死亡正是罪所带来的结局。
然而，神奇妙的作为也在这里显明出来：祂使属祂的人从死里复活，使他们不再沿着罪的方向无限坠落，而是在恩典中被更新，向着基督的样式生长，直到“满有基督长成的身量”。
这一切奇妙的作为，可以用两个字来称呼：
**福音。**
所以我们当然应当称谢神。
不是因为今生的一切供应不重要，而是因为这些恩典都不能与神在基督里所成就的救恩相比。神预备，神成就；神开始，神完成。
因此，我要一心称谢耶和华；
我要传扬祂一切奇妙的作为。
### The Wonderful Works of God Are the Gospel
What we give thanks for and proclaim is precisely the salvation that God has prepared for His chosen people and has Himself accomplished.
For He is the Beginning and the End. He does not become “the End” merely because something has finally been completed. He is the End because He is eternally who He is.
We often have to wait until an action or a project has come to an end before we can know its final result. But our Lord is not like this. The history of redemption has a beginning, a process, and an end, yet God Himself is not limited by that process. He knows the end from the beginning and carries all things out according to His eternal purpose.
If God Himself were limited, or if Christ were merely a finite creature, then Christ could not bear and accomplish God's eternal purpose of redemption. A creature may carry out a command that it has received, but this does not make that creature God.
Christ can accomplish salvation perfectly not because He is merely a creature of extraordinary power, but because He is the eternal Son of God, of the same essence as the Father. The salvation He accomplishes is the work of the Triune God Himself.
The problem with man is not that man's mind is itself infinite. Man is a creature, and therefore his reason, knowledge, and abilities remain finite. Yet man possesses the capacity continually to know, think, pursue, and explore, and the human heart can continually reach beyond immediate created things toward greater and higher objects. In this we see that man truly bears the image of God.
Yet sin has disordered the human will.
The fallen angels are also creatures; they are not infinite. Yet the intellect and will they originally received could be directed toward the wrong object. The same is true of man. Man did not sin because he possessed some infinite nature. Rather, while remaining a finite creature, he sought to cross the boundary God had established and desired to become “like God.”
The temptation in Eden began precisely here:
“When you eat of it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil.”
Then desire, when it has conceived, gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is fully grown, gives birth to death.
Thus Adam and Eve did not suddenly pass from life into death only after eating the fruit. Sin had a process within the human will—from temptation, to desire, to consent, and finally to actual disobedience—and death was the consequence of that sin.
Yet God's wonderful work is revealed precisely here: He raises His people from death and renews them by His grace, so that they no longer continue endlessly in the direction of sin, descending into an abyss, but instead grow toward the likeness of Christ, until they attain “the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ.”
All these wonderful works can be summed up in one word:
**the Gospel.**
Therefore, we ought indeed to give thanks to God.
Not because the blessings and provisions of this present life are unimportant, but because none of them can be compared with the salvation God has accomplished in Christ. God prepares; God accomplishes. God begins; God completes.
Therefore I will give thanks to the LORD with all my heart;
I will tell of all His wonderful works.

“我要因祢欢喜快乐。”
是因为什么呢？
因为祢的救恩，是的。
因为祢所赐的平安和安息，也是的。
但我想，更深的是因为祢把生命赐给了我。
不是仅仅把今生的生命延长，也不只是把一个不会死亡的生命赐给我，
乃是使我在基督里得着新的生命。
保罗说：
“基督是我们的生命。”（西 3:4）
那么，我若仍旧只是活在自己里面，即便我的生命能够无限延长，我又能快乐到哪里呢？
若只是我自己的生命不断延续，只是把今生的一切推向无穷，那么“永远”本身并不能成为永生。
因为我的问题，不只是生命太短，
而是我这个生命本身处在罪、死亡、忧愁、痛苦、背叛、误会和软弱之中。
即便人的肉体可以延寿数十年，甚至翻倍，并且一直保持壮年的体态，这仍然不能解决问题。
更何况，人心中所承受的，并不只是肉体的衰老。
我们还承受记忆。
那些忧愁、痛苦、背叛、误会、谩骂，以及我们所见、所听、所经历的一切苦难，都会进入我们的生命。
那么，假如我仍然是我自己，只是把这样的生命无限延长，我真的能够永远活下去吗？
即便我的生命中充满快乐，我又怎能完全不被世上的苦难所触动？
所以，真正的问题或许不是：
“人的生命能不能延长到永远？”
而是：
“这样的生命，是否就是永生？”
如果所谓永生，只是今生生命的无限延续，那么它似乎仍然只是“延长的今生”，而不是圣经所说的永生。
因此，永生必然涉及生命本身的更新。
可是，这里又出现了另一个问题：
新的生命怎样仍然是“我”的生命？
如果过去的一切都被抹去，
如果那些记忆、经历、历史全部消失，
那么新生命与旧生命之间还有怎样的连续性？
我若完全不认识自己的过去，我还是过去的那个“我”吗？
所以，新生命似乎既不能只是旧生命的无限延长，
也不能成为一个与过去毫无关系的全新个体。
它必须仍然是我，
却又不再是那个被罪、死亡和败坏支配的我。
它必须知道自己的来时路，
却不再被那条路所辖制。
它必须记得，
却不再被记忆所审判。
它必须仍然是我，
却真正活在一种新的生命里。
所以，复活所带来的，并不是历史被抹去，
而是人的整个生命被更新。
过去仍然属于我，
但过去不再成为我的主宰。
我仍然是我，
但我不再只是活在自己里面。
“基督是我们的生命。”
或许永生真正的奥秘就在这里：
不是我自己的生命终于获得了无限的时间，
而是我在基督里得着生命，
并且永远活在祂里面。
因此，
“我要因祢欢喜快乐”，
最终并不只是因为祢赐给我平安，
也不只是因为祢赐给我永生，
乃是因为祢自己就是我的生命，
也是我的欢喜快乐。

“I will rejoice and be glad in You.”
Why?
Because of Your salvation, yes.
Because of the peace and rest You give, yes.
But I think something deeper is involved:
You have given me life.
Not merely by extending the life I have in this world, and not simply by giving me a life that never dies,
but by giving me new life in Christ.
Paul says:
“Christ is our life.” (Col. 3:4)
Then if I were still living merely within myself, even if my life could be extended forever, where could I ultimately find joy?
If it were only my own life continuing endlessly, merely pushing everything in this present life into infinity, then “forever” itself would not yet be eternal life.
Because the problem with my life is not simply that it is too short.
The problem is that this life itself exists under sin, death, sorrow, suffering, betrayal, misunderstanding, and weakness.
Even if human beings could extend their physical lives by decades, or even double them, while maintaining the appearance of youth, that still would not solve the deeper problem.
For what we carry is not merely physical aging.
We also carry memory.
Sorrow, suffering, betrayal, misunderstanding, insults, and everything we have seen, heard, and experienced enter into our lives.
Then suppose I remained exactly myself, with nothing more than this kind of life extended forever. Could I truly live forever?
Even if my own life were filled with happiness, how could I remain untouched by the suffering of the world?
So perhaps the real question is not:
“Can human life be extended forever?”
but:
“Is this kind of life itself eternal life?”
If eternal life were merely the endless continuation of this present life, then it would seem to be only “this life extended,” rather than the eternal life spoken of in Scripture.
Therefore, eternal life must involve the renewal of life itself.
But this raises another question:
How can the new life still be my life?
If everything from the past were erased,
if all those memories, experiences, and histories disappeared,
what continuity would there be between the old life and the new?
If I had no knowledge at all of my past, would I still be the same “I”?
So the new life cannot simply be the old life endlessly extended,
nor can it be an entirely new individual unrelated to the past.
It must still be me,
yet I must no longer be the me who is ruled by sin, death, and corruption.
I must know where I came from,
yet I must no longer be enslaved by that past.
I must remember,
yet I must no longer be condemned by my memories.
I must still be myself,
yet truly live in a new kind of life.
Therefore, what resurrection brings is not the erasure of history,
but the renewal of the whole person.
The past still belongs to me,
but it is no longer my master.
I am still myself,
but I no longer live merely within myself.
“Christ is our life.”
Perhaps this is where the mystery of eternal life truly lies:
It is not that my own life finally receives an infinite amount of time,
but that I receive life in Christ,
and live forever in Him.
Therefore,
“I will rejoice and be glad in You”
ultimately means not merely that You give me peace,
nor merely that You give me eternal life,
but that You Yourself are my life,
and You Yourself are my joy and gladness.

而承接这新生命的，并不是旧有的生命；旧有的生命本身没有这样的能力，也不能成为承载这新生命的器皿。
承接这新生命的，乃是信心。
但这信心也并不是出于我自己。它不是我凭着自己的能力产生出来，好使我能够抓住基督；乃是基督自己所赐的，是祂在我里面所开始的，也是祂亲自成全的。
正如《希伯来书》所说：
“仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣。”（来12:2）
所以，若说信心是承接新生命的器皿，也必须说，这器皿本身也是基督所造的。
信心开始的时候，也许极其微小，如同一粒芥菜种；但它既然出于基督，既然是祂在我们里面开始的工作，就不会永远停留在那一点微小之中。
它虽小，却是真实的生命联系。
它开始时似乎只是一点微弱的信靠，一点对基督的依附，一点向着祂的仰望；但基督既然是这信心的创始者，也是成终者，那么这信心终必被祂带到完全。
因此，承接永生的，并不是我旧有生命的容量，
也不是我的理性能够理解多少，
不是我的记忆能够承受多少，
甚至也不是我的能力能够持守多少，
而是神使我借着信心与基督联合。
信心不是另一种生命，
基督才是生命。
信心只是使我归向祂、领受祂、住在祂里面的方式。
所以，我真正承受的，不是某种脱离基督而存在的“永生”，
乃是基督自己。
而这信心虽然开始时如同一粒芥菜种，却终将在基督里长成祂所要成就的生命。
---
And the one who receives this new life is not the old life itself. The old life does not possess such a capacity, nor can it serve as a vessel capable of containing this new life.
What receives this new life is faith.
Yet this faith does not originate from me either. It is not something I produce by my own ability so that I might be capable of grasping Christ. Rather, it is given by Christ Himself. He begins it within me, and He Himself brings it to completion.
As Hebrews says:
“Looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith.” (Heb. 12:2)
Therefore, even if faith is described as the vessel through which new life is received, that vessel itself must also be understood as something Christ creates.
Faith may begin very small, like a mustard seed. Yet because it comes from Christ, and because it is His work begun within us, it will not remain forever in that small state.
Though small, it is a real bond with life.
At first, it may seem to be nothing more than a weak trust, a small dependence upon Christ, a simple turning of the eyes toward Him. But since Christ is both the author and the finisher of this faith, He will surely bring it toward its completion.
Therefore, what receives eternal life is not the capacity of my old life,
nor how much my reason can understand,
nor how much my memory can contain,
nor even how much my own strength can preserve,
but the union with Christ that God brings about through faith.
Faith is not another kind of life.
Christ Himself is the life.
Faith is the means by which I turn toward Him, receive Him, and abide in Him.
So what I ultimately receive is not some form of “eternal life” existing apart from Christ,
but Christ Himself.
And although this faith begins like a mustard seed, it will ultimately grow, in Christ, into the fullness of the life He Himself intends to accomplish.

## 9:2

世间有许多美好的、可爱的、可喜悦的事物，是因为它们能够带给我们欢喜快乐。
新婚，
新生儿，
新房，
新车，
娱乐节目，
财产和收入……
我们因着它们而欢喜。
可是，这些事物里面，很少有同时以我们为自己的欢喜快乐。
恋人和新婚夫妻或许可以如此。
父母爱自己的婴幼儿，婴幼儿也以父母为自己的喜乐。
可是孩子渐渐长大以后，会有自己的世界、自己的爱、自己的选择，甚至有一天可能厌弃自己的父母和亲人。
娱乐节目不会喜欢我们。
我们喜欢偶像、艺人，
但他们通常并不认识我们，也不是在爱我们这个人。
他们所需要的，是我们的关注、订阅、购买和付费。
于是我们喜欢他们，
他们却未必喜欢我们。
想到这里，我甚至要问：
我们所爱的这些东西，真的是我们所爱的吗？
还是因为它们能够给我们带来快乐，所以我们才爱它们？
两者并不完全相同。
甚至，我们与宠物之间的关系，有时反而比人与许多所谓的关系更加接近一种彼此的喜爱。
人常说“狗不嫌家贫”，
它所给予的依恋，甚至胜过我们在人世间许多关系中所经历的爱。
那么，恋人和夫妻之间的恩爱又能够持续多久呢？
新鲜感能够持续多久？
彼此的陪伴能够持续多久？
这种关系又能够持续多久地成为彼此生命中的滋养？
没有人能够对这些事情真正有把握。
即便有人一生恩爱，
又能在一起多久呢？
这反倒成为一件令人悲哀的事情：
越是恩爱，
越是能够感受到岁月的飞逝；
越是珍惜，
越是害怕生离死别。
所以，如果诗人在这里所说的“欢喜快乐”，与世上的欢喜快乐没有本质上的区别，
那么这句话的意义就仍然有限。
因为世上的快乐常常只是：
“我喜欢你，因为你使我快乐。”
却未必是：
“我喜欢你，而你也以我为喜乐。”
如果不是相互的，
如果不是彼此知道的，
如果不是彼此都愿意以对方为自己的喜悦，
那么其中仍然带着某种不完全。
它甚至可能成为单相思。
我爱你，
你却不爱我。
你知道我爱你，
甚至喜欢我爱你，
可是你并没有像我爱你那样爱我。
那么，我爱你越深，
我的悲伤也可能越深。
因为我知道你并不以我为你的喜乐，
或者至少没有像我以你为喜乐那样以我为喜乐。
于是爱与失落同时存在。
而这恰恰使我想到我们与神之间的关系。
我们常常爱世界过于爱神。
即便我们说爱神，
也常常不如神所爱我们。
我们甚至会带着自己的企图来爱神：
为了平安，
为了保守，
为了财物，
为了健康，
为了永生。
我们说我们爱神，
却常常更爱神所赐给我们的东西。
可是，
“我要因祢欢喜快乐。”
这里的欢喜快乐若是真实的，
就不仅仅是：
“祢能够给我欢喜快乐。”
而是：
“祢自己就是我的欢喜快乐。”
并且更宝贵的是：
祢知道我因祢欢喜快乐，
祢也喜悦我以祢为我的欢喜快乐。
祢愿意我更多地以祢为我的喜乐。
而祢所赐的喜乐不是有限的，
不是随着岁月衰残的，
不是因为死亡而终止的，
也不是因为我所爱的其他事物失去而随之消失的。
祢能够无限地将祢自己赐给我，
并且祢所赐的，都是对我真正有益的，
都是最好的。
那么，我还有什么比这更宝贵的呢？
欢喜，
相互的欢喜，
被神所知的欢喜，
蒙神所喜悦的欢喜，
无限的欢喜，
永不失去的欢喜。
这就是为什么诗人说：
“我要因你欢喜快乐；
至高者啊，我要歌颂你的名！”
而这最终使我想到主耶稣所说的话：
“但是不可少的只有一件；马利亚已经选择那上好的福分，是不能夺去的。”（路10:42）
那不可少的，只有一件。
上好的福分，不能夺去。
因为最终，
我所要的不是祢所赐的一切，
乃是祢自己。
There are many beautiful, lovely, and delightful things in this world that bring us joy and gladness.
A new marriage,
a newborn child,
a new home,
a new car,
entertainment,
wealth and income…
We rejoice in these things because they bring us happiness.
Yet very few of these things also take us as their own source of joy.
Perhaps lovers and newly married couples can experience something like this.
Parents love their little children,
and little children also find their joy in their parents.
But as children grow older, they develop their own worlds, their own loves, and their own choices. One day, they may even reject their parents or other family members.
Entertainment programs do not love us.
We may love our favorite artists or celebrities,
but they usually do not know us, nor do they love us as persons.
What they need from us is our attention, subscriptions, purchases, and payments.
We may love them,
but they may not love us.
And when I think about this, I have to ask:
Do we really love all these things that we say we love?
Or do we love them because they make us happy?
The two are not necessarily the same.
Sometimes, even the relationship between a person and a pet seems closer to mutual affection than many of the relationships we call human relationships.
People say that a dog does not despise a poor home.
The affection a dog gives may sometimes seem more faithful than the love we experience in many human relationships.
Then how long can the love between lovers or married couples really last?
How long can the freshness remain?
How long can their companionship continue to nourish their lives?
And how long can such a relationship remain a source of joy for both?
No one can have complete certainty about these things.
Even if two people remain deeply loving toward one another throughout their lives,
how long can they remain together?
This itself becomes a sorrowful thing:
The deeper the love,
the more deeply we feel the passing of time.
The more precious the relationship,
the more we become aware of separation and death.
Therefore, if the “joy and gladness” spoken of by the psalmist is essentially no different from the happiness we experience from things in this world,
then its significance would still be limited.
For worldly happiness often takes the form of:
“I love you because you make me happy.”
But it may not be:
“I love you, and you also delight in me.”
If it is not mutual,
if it is not known by both,
if both do not willingly take delight in one another,
then something remains incomplete.
It may even become unrequited love.
I love you,
but you do not love me.
You know that I love you,
and perhaps you even enjoy being loved by me,
but you do not love me in the same way that I love you.
Then the deeper my love becomes,
the deeper my sorrow may also become.
Because I know that you do not take delight in me,
or at least you do not delight in me as deeply as I delight in you.
Love and loss exist together.
And this makes me think about our relationship with God.
We often love the world more than we love God.
Even when we say that we love God,
our love is often far less than the love with which God loves us.
We may even love God with our own hidden purposes:
for peace,
for protection,
for material provision,
for health,
for eternal life.
We say that we love God,
but often we love what God gives us more than we love God Himself.
Yet:
“I will rejoice and be glad in You.”
If this joy is truly real,
then it does not merely mean:
“You can give me joy.”
It means:
“You Yourself are my joy.”
And something even more precious is this:
You know that I rejoice in You,
and You delight in my taking You as my joy.
You desire that I would delight in You more and more.
And the joy You give is not limited.
It does not fade with time.
It does not end with death.
It does not disappear when the other things I love are taken away.
You can give Yourself to me without limit,
and what You give is truly good for me,
indeed, the very best.
Then what could possibly be more precious than this?
Joy,
mutual joy,
joy known by God,
joy in which God delights,
infinite joy,
joy that can never be taken away.
This is why the psalmist says:
“I will rejoice and be glad in You;
I will sing praise to Your name, O Most High.”
And this finally brings me to what Jesus said:
“But one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her.” (Luke 10:42)
One thing is necessary.
The good portion cannot be taken away.
Because in the end,
what I desire is not everything You can give me,
but You Yourself.

## 9:3-8

人常常按照自己的判断行事，也按照自己的判断评价自己。
我们可能认为自己所做的是正确的，甚至良心也不觉得有错；然而，人的判断并不能成为最终的标准。
正如保罗所说：
> “我被你们论断，或被别人论断，我都以为极小的事；连我自己也不论断自己。我虽不觉得自己有错，却也不能因此得以称义；但判断我的乃是主。”（哥林多前书4:3-4）
人的良心可以见证某些事情，却不能成为称义的依据。因为人无法完全看见自己的内心，也无法认识所有隐藏的动机。
唯有神坐在审判的宝座上。祂的判断不是按照人的自义，也不是按照人的自我辩护，而是按照祂自己的公义和正直。
因此，人最大的危险，不只是做错事，而是在错误中仍以为自己是对的；不是没有判断，而是把自己的判断放在神的判断之上。
---
People often act according to their own judgment and evaluate themselves according to their own standards.
We may believe that what we have done is right, and even our conscience may not accuse us. However, human judgment cannot become the final standard.
As Paul said:
> “I care very little if I am judged by you or by any human court; indeed, I do not even judge myself. My conscience is clear, but that does not make me innocent. It is the Lord who judges me.” (1 Corinthians 4:3-4)
Human conscience may testify concerning certain matters, but it cannot become the basis of justification. For people cannot fully see their own hearts, nor can they fully know all their hidden motives.
Only God sits upon the throne of judgment. His judgment is not according to human self-righteousness or human self-defense, but according to His own righteousness and uprightness.
Therefore, the greatest danger for mankind is not only doing what is wrong, but believing oneself to be right while being wrong; not merely lacking judgment, but placing one's own judgment above God's judgment.

## 9:9

倘若欺压我的，是人，是某种势力，
我会因此痛苦、难受，
甚至受到几乎非人的折磨。
然而，这些都不能真正把我下到地狱里。
因为人的手能够伤害我的身体，
能够夺去我的财物，
能够羞辱我，
甚至能够夺去今世的生命，
却不能成为我永恒结局的主宰。
真正可怕的，是欺压我的、掳掠我的乃是罪。
若是罪辖制我，
那我真是苦啊。
因为我所受的，就不只是罪所带来的痛苦，
我自己又活在罪的权势之下，
并且还承担罪的罪责。
罪的结局乃是死，
死后且有审判。
所以，主啊，
我真正所求的，不只是求祢救我脱离人的欺压，
更是求祢的救恩，
使我脱离罪的权势，
使我不再作罪的奴仆，
乃是在基督里得着释放，
归给祢，
一生不再让罪作我的王。

If the one who oppresses me is a person,
or some earthly power,
I may suffer because of it,
I may be distressed,
and I may even endure treatment that seems almost inhuman.
Yet none of these things can truly cast me into hell.
For human hands may wound my body,
take away my possessions,
humiliate me,
and even take away my earthly life,
but they cannot determine my eternal destiny.
What is truly terrible is when the one who oppresses and enslaves me is sin.
If sin has dominion over me,
then indeed I am wretched.
For I suffer not only from the pain that sin brings,
but I also remain under the power of sin
and bear its guilt.
The end of sin is death,
and after death comes judgment.
Therefore, Lord,
what I truly ask is not merely that You deliver me from the oppression of people,
but that Your salvation would deliver me from the dominion of sin,
so that I may no longer be a slave to sin,
but be set free in Christ,
belonging wholly to You,
and no longer allow sin to reign as my king.

## 9:10

恶人，就是那些以自己为义、不以神为义的人。
按此标准，我们中间没有义人，全员都在行恶。
选民却是因神自己的名所拣选、所保守的一群，并非因为他们身上有任何可被拣选的品质。
荣耀只当归给神的名。
在神公义的审判面前，我们都站立不住。
我们所宝爱、所追求的一切——强权、金钱、势力、才华、能力——都不能使我们在这审判中刚强壮胆。
它们正如人呼叫大山倒在自己身上、小山遮盖自己时所依靠的那些事物；在公义的烈焰与风暴中，不过是禾秸与糠秕。
多少人为这些事物的失去而惋惜、感叹，
却无人因神的公义曾被藐视而生发义怒，
也无人因自己亏欠神的公义而感到痛苦。
我们还有多少时间与机会可以悔改？
我们欺压人、亏负人、轻慢人——神能不审问吗？
这里面不少正是我们的家人、亲人、朋友、会友。
焉知今天不是最后悔改的机会？
我们靠抢夺、偷窃、巧计所得的是什么？
若在主面前仍是贫穷的，我们终究一无所有。
因为连我们自己的生命、时间、健康，最终都无法留住。
我们并不是八福中那灵里贫穷的人。
我们只是肉体上贫穷、饥渴、贪婪的人；
在公义、真理与爱心上，我们一无所有，亏欠极大，却毫不察觉——
如同人在饥荒、瘟疫、地震、战争或灾祸临近时，仍浑然不觉。
---
The wicked are those who regard themselves as righteous and do not regard God as righteous.
By this measure, there is none righteous among us; all practice evil.
The elect, however, are a people chosen and preserved solely because of God’s own name, not because of any quality in them that could be selected.
Glory belongs to the name of God alone.
Before the righteous judgment of God, none of us can stand.
All that we treasure and pursue—power, wealth, influence, talent, ability—cannot give us courage in that judgment.
They are precisely the things for which people cry to the mountains to fall on them and the hills to cover them.
In the face of God’s righteous fire and storm they are only straw and chaff.
How many people feel regret and lament when these things are lost,
yet no one is moved to righteous anger that God’s justice has been despised,
and no one suffers over the debt owed to God’s justice.
How much time and opportunity remain for us to repent?
We oppress, defraud, and treat others with contempt—will God not inquire?
Among them are many of our own family, relatives, friends, and fellow believers.
Who knows whether today is the last opportunity to repent?
What do we gain by seizing, stealing, and scheming?
If we remain poor before the Lord, we still have nothing.
In the end we discover that even our own life, time, and health cannot be kept.
We are not the poor of the Beatitudes.
We are only the fleshly poor, hungry, and greedy;
in righteousness, truth, and love we possess nothing, owe a great debt, and remain unaware—
like those who sense nothing while famine, plague, earthquake, war, or disaster approaches.

## 9:11-13

主是我们的救主。祂拯救我们脱离死亡的权势和咒诅。
这死亡不只是将来永远的死亡，也就是被审判、落在阴间的结局；它也显明在今生人的生命之中。
今生的死亡，并不只是肉体的衰残，而是人的生命与神隔绝所产生的败坏。它表现在人的心思和行为中：
仇恨，骄傲，自以为是，自以为义，自作聪明，以及拒绝真理的心。
人虽然活着，却可能被这些罪所辖制，失去神所赐的生命。
因此，基督的救恩不仅是将来把人从永远的死亡中救出来，也是现在将人从罪的权势中释放，使人重新归向神，得着真正的生命。
---
The Lord is our Savior. He saves us from the power and curse of death.
This death is not only the eternal death that comes under God’s judgment and leads to the grave; it is also manifested in our present earthly life.
The death we experience in this life is not merely the decay of the body, but the corruption that comes from humanity’s separation from God. It is revealed through the condition of the heart and through our actions:
hatred, pride, self-righteousness, self-confidence, self-deception, and the refusal to submit to the truth.
A person may be physically alive while being controlled by these sins and losing the life that God intended to give.
Therefore, Christ’s salvation is not only about rescuing people from eternal death in the future; it is also about delivering them from the power of sin now, restoring them to God, and giving them true life.

## 9:14-16

我们一生之中，重要的，或许并不只是我们做什么，也包括我们不做什么。
这并不是说，我们可以放弃那些本当承担的责任。
我们当敬拜耶和华我们的神，当孝敬父母，当尽我们所当尽的责任。
可是，我们的问题在于：即使在那些本来应当做的事情上，我们也并不能靠自己做好。
不是说我们偶尔做得不够好，而是说，若离开神的恩典，我们甚至没有真正行善的能力。
我们当信神。
可是，连与耶稣同船的门徒，面对风浪时，也显出了他们的不信。
面对收丁税的人，彼得也不知道应当怎样回答。
面对大祭司使女的质疑，彼得甚至发咒起誓，说自己不认识那人。
嗐，如果连这些都做不到，
我们又凭什么相信自己能做什么呢？
然后，我们许多正在做的、急着做的、想要做的，
又有多少不是在为自己挖坑、编网、埋雷？
最后，缠住我们的，
也许正是我们自己的手所做的。
如此说来，我们这样忙碌，究竟是为了什么？
除去那些为衣食所不得不有的劳碌，
我们究竟为什么劳心？
我们所急着完成的，究竟是什么？
有些事情，不但不能使我们得益处，
反而会使我们受亏损。
更可怕的是，
它们还可能使我们自己，或者使别人，陷在罪里。
我们有祸了，因为我们犯罪得罪神。
我们也有祸了，因为我们自己竟成了那使人跌倒的人。
所以，我们需要在神面前安静。
承认自己所不能看见的罪，
承认自己以为能够做、却终究做不成的善，
忏悔，求怜悯，求恩惠，
在祂公义的愤怒以前。
因为我们所需要的，
并不是继续相信自己的手能够解决什么，
而是先知道，我们的手已经做了什么。
我们不应当只忙碌于眼前的忙碌，
仿佛我们要在这地上永远活下去。
有些时候，
停下来，
并不是因为我们已经做够了，
而是因为我们终于开始害怕：
自己若继续凭着自己而做，
究竟还要为自己掘多少坑，
又要编多少网，
并且最终，
被自己手所做的缠住。
Perhaps what matters in the course of our lives is not only what we do, but also what we do not do.
This does not mean that we may abandon the responsibilities that properly belong to us.
We ought to worship the LORD our God. We ought to honor our parents. We ought to fulfill the responsibilities entrusted to us.
Yet our problem is this: even in the things we ought to do, we cannot truly do them well by our own strength.
It is not merely that we sometimes fail to do them well enough. Rather, apart from the grace of God, we do not even possess the ability to do true good.
We ought to believe God.
Yet even the disciples who were in the boat with Jesus revealed their unbelief when they faced the storm.
When confronted by those who collected the temple tax, Peter did not know how he ought to answer.
And when questioned by the servant girl of the high priest, Peter even cursed and swore that he did not know the man.
Ah, if we cannot even do these things,
then what can we possibly trust ourselves to do?
And among the many things we are doing, rushing to do, and longing to do,
how many are actually pits we are digging for ourselves, nets we are weaving, or traps we are burying?
In the end, what entangles us
may be the very work of our own hands.
If that is so, what are we so busy for?
Apart from the labor necessary for food and clothing,
what is it that we truly trouble our minds over?
What is it that we are so eager to accomplish?
Some things do not merely fail to benefit us.
They bring us loss.
And more terribly,
they may cause either ourselves or others to fall into sin.
Woe to us, because we sin and offend God.
And woe to us when we ourselves become those through whom others stumble.
Therefore, we need to become quiet before God.
To acknowledge the sins we cannot see,
to acknowledge the good we think we are capable of doing, yet ultimately cannot accomplish,
to repent, to seek mercy, and to ask for grace,
before His righteous wrath.
For what we need
is not to continue trusting that our own hands can solve everything,
but first to know what our hands have already done.
We should not merely remain occupied with the busyness before our eyes,
as though we were going to live forever on this earth.
Sometimes,
to stop
is not because we have already done enough,
but because we have finally begun to fear
how many more pits we may dig for ourselves
and how many more nets we may weave
if we continue acting according to ourselves—
until, in the end,
we are entangled
by the work of our own hands.

## 9:17-20

罪是令人羞耻的。
所以犯罪之后，亚当夏娃用无花果树的叶子编作裙子，遮盖羞耻。
这里的羞耻，在更重要的意义上，不是因为身体的裸露。
的确，从那时起人开始有对身体裸露的羞耻，并且这种羞耻感一代一代传了下来。即便一个平常不介意裸露的人，若被勉强在大庭广众之下露出身体，多半仍会觉得羞耻——十字架上的受刑者就是一例。
但身体裸露的羞耻在这段叙事里不是重点。除了夫妻二人，当时没有其他人。即便今天，若确定周围没有其他人，人通常也不特别介意自己是否衣不蔽体。当然也有对身体羞耻感很强的人，明知无人闯入，仍更愿意穿好衣服。
在更重要的意义上，这里的羞耻是罪的羞耻。
罪与上帝的旨意相违背。与上帝的旨意相违背，就是罪的产生。
罪的母是私欲；所谓私欲，是与上帝的旨意相违背的欲求。
神是光，是爱，是公义，是良善，是信实，是真理。
因此，与神的旨意相违背的，不可能是另一种光、爱、公义、良善、信实、真理，或次一等的这些，而只能是它们的相反：黑暗、恨、不公义、邪恶、说谎、谬误。
黑暗在黑暗中，恨在怨恨中，不公义在不公义的地方，邪恶在凶恶的地方，说谎在谎言中，谬误在错谬中，往往不觉得羞耻，因为大家都一样，甚至觉得自己比别人还好一点。乌鸦觉得自己没有黑猪那么黑——至少自己身上黑色的面积，未必比得上黑猪两只耳朵加起来。于是自我感觉不错，更不觉得羞耻。
但在神所设立的良心面前，人无法长久躲避、隐藏或欺骗；即便能暂时欺骗自己的良心，也不能持久。
在察看人肺腑心肠的神面前，这一切是赤露敞开、无法隐藏的。这是始祖的羞耻。
这种羞耻或者带来遮掩——用无花果树的叶子编作裙子。
这种羞耻不在于对罪行的懊悔，而在于对审判者——真光——的逃避，甚至厌恶、憎恶。在这种羞耻里，人更加恨恶神。
这种羞耻或者带来悔改——除非救恩临到。
这种羞耻带来对罪行的恨恶，是自恨，能生出没有后悔的懊悔来。
前一种羞耻的标志是遮掩、推诿、自我开脱，甚至嫁祸于人、转移问题。并不求所得罪之人的饶恕，更不求神的赦免之恩。始祖遮掩之后彼此推诿，该隐先推掉看守兄弟的责任、随后只诉刑罚太重，扫罗一面说「我有罪了」、一面把原因推给百姓、又求在人面前得体面，都落在这一类里。
后一种羞耻的标志是：不但以罪或过失为羞耻，也以遮掩罪、遮掩过失为羞耻。只觉自己所当得的，是像十字架上那样赤身被公开处刑。不但求所得罪之人的饶恕，更求神的赦免之恩。十字架上其中一个强盗说：「我们是应该的，因我们所受的与我们所做的相称。……耶稣啊，你得国降临的时候，求你记念我。」
---
#### **Sin and Shame**
Sin is shameful.
After they sinned, therefore, Adam and Eve sewed fig leaves together and made coverings to hide their shame.
In the more important sense, the shame here is not the shame of bodily nakedness.
It is true that a sense of shame at bodily nakedness began then and has been handed down from generation to generation. Even someone who ordinarily does not mind being unclothed would likely still feel shame if forced to stand naked before a crowd—as those hung on a cross did.
But the shame of bodily nakedness is not the point of this narrative. Apart from the man and the woman, no one else was there. Even now, when a person is sure that no other human being is present, he or she usually does not much mind being uncovered. There are, of course, people in whom bodily shame is strong enough that they would rather remain clothed even in a place they know no one will enter.
In the more important sense, the shame here is the shame of sin.
Sin is what stands against the will of God. What stands against the will of God is how sin arises.
The mother of sin is inordinate desire; and inordinate desire, so called, is desire that stands against the will of God.
God is light, love, righteousness, goodness, faithfulness, and truth.
What stands against his will cannot be another light, or a lesser love, righteousness, goodness, faithfulness, or truth. It can only be their opposites: darkness, hatred, injustice, evil, lying, and error.
Darkness in the dark, hatred among the resentful, injustice where injustice is at home, evil among the vicious, lying among lies, error among errors—there people often feel no shame, because everyone is the same, and some even think themselves a little better than the rest. The crow thinks it is not as black as the black pig; at least the black on its own body may not add up to the pig’s two ears. So it feels rather well about itself, and feels no shame at all.
But before the conscience God has set in the human being, a person cannot hide, evade, or deceive for long. Even self-deception of conscience does not last.
Before the God who searches heart and mind, all of this lies open and cannot be covered. That is the shame of the first parents.
This shame may lead to covering—sewing fig leaves into a skirt.
Such shame does not consist in grief over the deed. It consists in flight from the Judge—the true light—even in aversion and loathing. In this shame a person hates God more.
Or this shame may lead to repentance—unless salvation comes.
That shame produces hatred of the sin itself, a hatred of oneself, and can give rise to a repentance that is not regretted.
The marks of the first shame are covering, blame-shifting, self-exculpation, even pinning the fault on someone else and moving the question elsewhere. It does not ask pardon from the person who was wronged, and still less asks God for the grace of forgiveness. After they covered themselves, the first parents shifted blame onto each other. Cain first refused the charge of being his brother’s keeper, then pleaded only that the penalty was too heavy. Saul said, “I have sinned,” then laid the cause on the people and asked to be honored before them. All of these fall in this class.
The marks of the second shame are these: it is ashamed not only of the sin or the fault, but also of having covered the sin or the fault. It takes public naked execution on a cross to be what is due. It asks pardon from the person who was wronged, and still more asks God for the grace of forgiveness. One of the robbers on the cross said, “We are receiving what our deeds deserve. … Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom.”
