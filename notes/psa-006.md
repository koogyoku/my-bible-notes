# 诗篇 第6章

## 6:1

> 在神的面前悔改，远胜于在世上赚取名利。

> 唯有神的慈爱怜悯能更新并改变我的心意，其他任何都无能为力。

> 你知道我的软弱：我对行恶聪慧，对行善却愚拙。

> 在恶事上聪明，在善事上愚笨。

> 我既无力自救，又怎能救人？

> 若我自以为是“医生”，我真能医治自己吗？

> Repenting before God is far better than gaining fame and fortune in the world.

> Only God’s love and mercy can renew and transform my mind; nothing else can.

> You know my weakness: I am clever in doing evil but foolish in doing good.

> Clever in evil, foolish in good.

> Since I am powerless to save myself, how can I save others?

> If I regard myself as a “doctor,” can I truly heal myself?

> 祢要向我发怒，原本出于祢的公义和圣洁。
> 祢已经向我忍耐，施慈爱，祢并不亏欠我，
> 反倒我在罪恶中从不醒悟悔改。
> 我能说什么呢？我能埋怨祢吗？能责怪祢吗？能质问祢为何向我发怒吗？

> 我本是罪人所生的罪人，罪人中的罪魁。
> 若论狡诈，我比众人有过之而无不及。
> 若论正直，我只能自欺。
> 当祢怒气来临时，我还要紧紧抓住属地的好处吗？
> 当祢鉴察我时，我仍像牲畜没有灵性而自觉羞愧吗？

> 然而，我宁愿祢的怒气如火焰吞灭我，
> 也不要用世俗的快乐麻痹自己。
> 正像我主基督在十字架上并不受苦胆调和的酒。
> 我承受祢这些怒气还算小事，
> 因为祢大有慈爱和怜悯，祢知道我是极其软弱，
> 我是虫，不是人。
> 我有聪明行恶，却无智慧行善。

> Your anger toward me arises from Your righteousness and holiness.
> You have already been patient with me and shown me steadfast love; You owe me nothing.
> On the contrary, I have remained in sin without awakening to repentance.
> What can I say? Can I complain against You? Can I blame You? Can I question why You are angry with me?

> I am a sinner born of sinners, the chief of sinners.
> In deceit I surpass others by far.
> In uprightness I can only deceive myself.
> When Your anger comes, should I still cling tightly to earthly advantages?
> When You examine me, should I still be like a beast without understanding and feel ashamed?

> Yet I would rather Your anger consume me like fire
> than numb myself with worldly pleasures.
> Just as my Lord Christ on the cross did not receive the wine mixed with gall.
> The anger I bear from You is still a small matter,
> because You are great in steadfast love and mercy; You know that I am extremely weak—
> I am a worm, not a man.
> I have cleverness to do evil, but no wisdom to do good.

## 6:2

> 我宁愿你向我发怒，也不愿你向我掩面；
> 求你不要弃绝我，如同弃绝该隐、以扫，或像对待扫罗王那样。
> 我宁愿在你的怒气中挣扎受苦，也不愿在世俗的欢愉满足里自我陶醉。

> 因此，我不羡慕这世上任何的快乐——无论王位、尊荣、掌声、赞扬、财富、子孙或享乐。
> 我愿意聆听你的指责，而不愿如亚当夏娃那样为自己辩护。

> 你知道我的本相，我比同时代的人更加软弱：缺乏分辨，没有机智，不知趋福避祸。
> 我宁愿承受你的怒气，也不愿像他们一样，在肉体的今生满足中自夸自矜，或沉溺于他人的夸赞羡慕。
> 主啊，求你不要让我落到那样的地步。

> 求你将我带到你的宝座前，指出我每一个过错，不要让我离开你的面。
> 因为我实在软弱，没有自救的能力。
> 若不是你为我预备救恩，我本不可能悔改归向你，只会硬着颈项远离你，与你为敌。

> 因此，在救恩之事上，我毫无能力或智慧可言；
> 在仇敌面前，我也毫无辨别或还手之力。
> 任凭仇敌吞吃我，我竟不能自救逃脱。
> 你的诫命已显明我的问题。

> 所以，求你怜悯我，医治我，
> 使我从瞎眼中得见救恩，从耳聋中得闻真道，
> 从哑巴中得开口祷告，从瘸腿瘫痪中得健步行走你的道路，
> 并从死亡中复活，得着复活的身体。

> I would rather You be angry with me than hide Your face from me;
> do not reject me as You rejected Cain and Esau, or as You dealt with King Saul.
> I would rather struggle and suffer in Your anger than intoxicate myself in the pleasures and satisfactions of this world.

> Therefore I do not envy any happiness in this world—whether throne, honor, applause, praise, wealth, descendants, or pleasure.
> I am willing to hear Your rebuke, and unwilling to defend myself as Adam and Eve defended themselves.

> You know my true condition; I am weaker than my contemporaries: lacking discernment, without cleverness, not knowing how to pursue blessing and avoid disaster.
> I would rather bear Your anger than be like them, boasting in the satisfaction of the flesh in this life, or indulging in the praise and admiration of others.
> Lord, do not let me fall into such a state.

> Bring me before Your throne and point out every one of my faults; do not let me depart from Your presence.
> For I am truly weak and have no ability to save myself.
> Were it not for the salvation You have prepared for me, I could not repent and turn to You, but would only harden my neck and go far from You, becoming Your enemy.

> Therefore, in the matter of salvation I have no ability or wisdom to speak of;
> before my enemies I have no power of discernment or ability to strike back.
> Should the enemy devour me, I would be utterly unable to save or escape myself.
> Your commandments have already exposed my problem.

> So have mercy on me and heal me,
> that from blind eyes I may see salvation, from deaf ears I may hear the true word,
> from a mute mouth I may open in prayer, from lame and paralyzed legs I may walk steadily in Your ways,
> and from all death I may be raised to life, possessing a resurrected body.

> 这里的“软弱”否定了我们在善上的一切努力、自律、勤奋与智慧，肯定了我们在善上的一无所是、一无所能。

> 你当然可以反驳：保罗不是向着标杆努力直奔吗？箴言不是叫懒惰人观看蚂蚁吗？基督不是说天国是努力进入的吗？

> 对这些反驳，我选择不与你们争辩，只为你们祷告。

> 但我自己深知毫无能力：若不是神施恩，且这恩典是全备的赏赐，我必定倒毙旷野。

> The “weakness” spoken of here negates all effort, self-discipline, diligence, and wisdom on our part in what is good, affirming that we are nothing and can do nothing in the realm of good.

> You may of course object: Did not Paul say he was pressing on toward the goal? Does not Proverbs warn the sluggard to consider the ways of the ant? Did not Christ say that the kingdom is entered through striving?

> To such objections I choose not to argue, but will pray for you instead.

> But for myself, I know I have no ability whatsoever: Were it not for God’s grace—and that grace a complete gift—I would surely perish in the wilderness.

## 6:3

> 使徒行传 5:31 神…将悔改的心和赦罪的恩赐给以色列人。

> 诚然如此。

> 如果没有赦罪的恩，就不会有悔改的心。

> 神为自己选民所预备的实在超出我们想象。

## 6:4-5

> 基督是父神慈爱的终极表达，
> 是神的“转回”。否则，在始祖犯罪后，就无人能得救。
> 神的慈爱，也是我生命的终极意义和价值所在——
> 也是基督。
> 这是我生命的标竿，奔跑的价值，头上的冠冕，项上的金链。
> 若不是神藉着基督的拯救，
> 我的结局就是死地和阴间。
> 在那里我怎能记念神，怎能称谢神？
> 如此说来，我能在活人之地记念祢，
> 称谢数算祢的恩典，
> 这不就是祢的慈爱临到了我，
> 这不就是祢极大的救恩吗？
> 因祢的救恩藉着慈爱临到，约拿在大鱼腹中也能向祢发出称谢。
> 但若祢稍微向我掩面，
> 连约拿也会仅仅因为蓖麻被虫咬干枯而发怨言。
> 所以，祢的慈爱，就是我在这人世间沙漠中的清凉所，
> 又是避难所。

> Christ is the ultimate expression of God the Father's steadfast love,
> and He is God's "turning back." Otherwise, after the fall of our first ancestors, no one could be saved.
> God's steadfast love is also the ultimate meaning and value of my life—
> which is also Christ.
> This is the mark of my life, the value of the race I run, the crown on my head, and the golden chain around my neck.
> Were it not for God's salvation through Christ,
> my end would be the realm of the dead and Sheol.
> How could I remember God there, and how could I give Him thanks?
> Thus speaking, the fact that I can remember You in the land of the living,
> giving thanks and counting Your grace,
> is this not Your steadfast love coming upon me,
> is this not Your great salvation?
> Because Your salvation comes through Your steadfast love, even Jonah could give thanks to You from the belly of the great fish.
> But if You hide Your face from me even slightly,
> even Jonah would complain merely because a plant was withered by a worm.
> Therefore, Your steadfast love is my place of coolness in the desert of this world,
> and also my refuge.

## 6:6-7

> 我们被罪压伤，为世上的一切思虑所烦扰——忧愁，不得安息。
> 我们如同在沙漠之地，找不到荫凉之处，
> 我的拯救从何而来？

> 我的罪如何在公义的神面前得除净？
> 我既然软弱、知罪，却无法脱离罪恶，
> 谁能解开我的捆锁？
> 谁能医治这灵魂的疾病？

> 唯有神有慈爱和怜悯。我独自思想并想要知道：
> 祂的救法究竟如何？
> 祂与亚伯拉罕所立的约，究竟如何实现？

> 细想这些，我属地的忧愁和思虑反倒算为小事。

> We are crushed by sin and disturbed by all the cares of this world—sorrow, and no rest.
> We are like those in a desert land, finding no shady place;
> where does my salvation come from?

> How can my sin be cleansed before the righteous God?
> Since I am weak and know my sin yet cannot escape from evil,
> who can break my bonds?
> Who can heal this disease of the soul?

> Only God has steadfast love and mercy. I ponder this alone and long to know:
> How does His way of salvation work?
> How is the covenant He made with Abraham fulfilled?

> When I think carefully on these things, my earthly sorrows and anxieties appear as small matters in comparison.

## 6:8-10

> 我要尽可能远远躲避一切人的聚集，
> 不管他们在商议什么。
> 那不过是互相激发罪孽，
> 无法在其中更加认识神，明白祂的旨意。
> 不去探听人的心思，
> 也不接受人的试探。
> 只愿意在神的面前不停止祈祷和代祷。
> 只愿意藉着圣经的话语不断认识神。
> 我知道祢听我的恳求，也收纳我的祷告，
> 因为我不求自己的意思，乃求祢的意思，
> 不求自己的荣耀，乃求祢的荣耀。
> 如此说来，这不分明是基督对父神的祷告吗？
> 这里作孽的人不是别人，正是我。
> 而我不要因为基督这样的批评而离开祂，
> 反倒恰恰因为祂如此的批评，
> 而知道我正是需要祂的救赎。

> “一切人的聚集”都会互相激发罪孽，这是我的论断。即便是最虔诚的信徒聚集在一起讨论神学和圣经真理，甚至是奥古斯丁或加尔文今日在世，我与他们聚集也是如此，这皆因我们的罪性使然。然而，这并非意味着全然不聚集——若是不聚，又如何举行圣餐、礼拜与洗礼呢？而是当我们不可避免要聚集时，必须先明确这些前提，从而将聚会的意义和目的界定清楚。若非必要，不当无谓聚集。我们的灵命，即对基督的认识，并非在聚集中成长，反倒是在我们独处在神面前的时候。正如基督所说：“你祷告的时候，要进你的内屋，关上门，祷告你在暗中的父。”

> 我想我们并非真正理解奥古斯丁或加尔文对聚会的看法。如果没有相当时间的独处，怎会有时间思考圣经的话语和神学议题？如果没有相当时间的独处，他们又怎能写下如此多的著作？正如保罗批评哥林多教会：“你们聚会不是受益，乃是招损”，因为他们聚会时彼此分门别类。

> I wish to try my best to stay far away from all gatherings of people,
> no matter what they are discussing.
> It is merely the mutual stirring of iniquity,
> wherein one cannot come to know God better or understand His will.
> I will not probe the minds of men,
> nor accept their temptations.
> I desire only to pray and intercede without ceasing before God.
> I desire only to know God continuously through the words of Scripture.
> I know You hear my plea and accept my prayer,
> because I do not seek my own will, but Yours;
> not my own glory, but Yours.
> Speaking thus, is this not clearly Christ’s prayer to God the Father?
> The worker of evil here is none other than myself.
> Yet I will not depart from Christ because of such rebuke;
> rather, precisely because of His rebuke,
> I know that I am the very one who needs His redemption.

> That "all gatherings of people" mutually stir up iniquity is my own assertion. Even when the most devout believers gather to discuss theology and biblical truth—or even if Augustine or Calvin were alive today and I were to gather with them—it would still be so, due to our inherent sinful nature. This, however, does not mean we should abandon gathering entirely; otherwise, how could we administer the Lord's Supper, worship, and baptism? Rather, when gathering is unavoidable, we must clearly understand these premises so that we may strictly define the purpose and meaning of our assembly. Apart from what is necessary, we should not gather. Our spiritual life—our knowledge of Christ—does not grow in gatherings, but rather when we stand alone before God. For Christ said, "But when you pray, go into your room, close the door and pray to your Father, who is unseen."

> I think we do not truly understand Augustine’s or Calvin’s views on gathering. Without a significant amount of solitude, how would there be time to contemplate the words of Scripture and theological matters? Without substantial time in solitude, how could they have written so many works? Just as Paul criticized the Corinthian church, saying that their meetings did more harm than good, because when they gathered, there were divisions among them.
